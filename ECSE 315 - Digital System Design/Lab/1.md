### a

What type of register is this? (Positive Edge-Triggered Register or Negative Edge-Triggered Register). Explain and Illustrate using Sedit and simulation waveform.

> [!answer]
> Positive edge triggered.
> 
> The first stage is negative edge trigged, but the second stage is positive edge triggered, meaning the circuit as a whole will be positive edge triggered.
> 
> When $CLK$ is low in the first stage. $D$ is able to open the input to $I1$ to be either $Vdd$ or $Gnd$. When $CLK$ is high, $D$ is blocked from altering the output of $I1$ and instead will feed the output of $I1$ back into itself inverted, keeping the signal frozen at whatever it was before the $CLK$ turned high.
> 
> The second stage is the opposite, allowing $I1$ to alter the output of $I2$ only if the $CLK$ is high, and looping the output back if the $CLK$ is low. This ultimately means the output will be whatever $D$ was at the beginning of the positive $CLK$ edge, before the inverters.
> 
> As seen in the graphics at the end of this document, the output only changes on positive edges of $CLK$.

### b

Assume that the propagation delay of each clocked inverter (e.g., M1-M4) is $T_{CLK\_INV}$ and the delay of inverters $I1$ and $I2$ is $T_{INV}$. Derive the expression for the set-up time ($T_{SETUP}$) the propagation delay ($T_{CLOCK-Q}$) and the hold time ($T_{HOLD}$) in terms of the above parameters. Explain your results.

> [!answer]
> $T_{SETUP}$ must be at least long enough for the clocked inverter to propagate, then the next falling clock cycle. If at the right time, $T_{SETUP}=T_{CLK\_INV}$ as any time after that, it could already be locked by the inverter.
> 
> $T_{CLOCK-Q}$ is limited only by the speed of the second clocked inverter and the final inverter, thus making its delay $T_{CLOCK-Q}=T_{CLK\_INV}+T_{INV}$.
> 
> $T_{HOLD}$ can be 0, as when it is locked after the setup time, the signal is already set and cannot change nor influence the output of that stage.

> As explained in part a, they exist to "lock" the output when the $CLK$ is not sampling the input to that stage. It loops the output of the inverter back as the input to the clocked inverter, which keeps the output the same as whatever it was before it was locked.

### a

What Boolean functions are implemented at outputs $F$ and $G$?

> [!answer]
> $F=(A0B0+A0'B0')=(A0\otimes B0)'$
> $G=F(A1B1+A1'B1')=F(A1\otimes B1)'=(A0\otimes B0)'(A1\otimes B1)'$

### b

If $A = (A1~A0)$ and $B = (B1~B0)$, then what interpretation can be applied to output $G$.

> [!answer]
> In that case, the formula for $G$ would simplify to $(A\otimes B)'$ as the product would be the same on both sides.
