## 1

A synthetic fiber used in manufacturing carpet has tensile strength that is normally distributed with mean $75.5$ psi and standard deviation $3.5$ psi. Find the probability that a random sample of $n = 6$ fiber specimens will have a sample mean tensile strength that exceeds $75.75$ psi.

Hint: Even though the sample size is only 6 you may assume that it is large enough to justify the use of the Central Limit Theorem.

> [!answer]
> $X\sim N(75.5,3.5^{2})$
> $\bar{X}\sim N\left(75.5, \frac{3.5^{2}}{6}\right)$ by the CLT for an average of $6$ samples
> We are finding $P(\bar{X}>75.75)$.
> $P(\bar{X}>75.75)=P(Z>\frac{75.75-75.5}{\sqrt{\frac{3.5^{2}}{6}}})=P(Z>0.1749635530559413)$
> $=P(Z<-0.1749635530559413)$ by $Z$ symmetry
> $=0.43055413611371507$ by normal CDF function

## 2

Suppose we have a random sample of size $2n$ from a population denoted by $X$, and $E(X) = \mu$ and $V (X) = \sigma^{2}$. Let

$$
\begin{align}
\bar{X_{1}}&=\frac{1}{2n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}X_{i}\\
\bar{X_{2}}&=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\\
\end{align}
$$

be two estimators of $\mu$. Which is the better estimator of $\mu$? Explain your choice.

> [!answer]
> $E(\bar{X_{1}})=E\left(\frac{1}{2n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}X_{i}\right)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}E(X_{i})$ assuming samples are independent
> $\frac{1}{2n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\mu=\frac{1}{2n}2n\mu=\mu$
> 
> $E(\bar{X_{2}})=E\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right)=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}E(X_{i})$ assuming samples are independent
> $\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mu=\frac{1}{n}n\mu=\mu$
> 
> $Var(\bar{X_{1}})=Var\left(\frac{1}{2n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}X_{i}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{2n}\right)^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}Var(X_{i})$ assuming samples are independent
> $\left(\frac{1}{2n}\right)^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\sigma^{2}=\left(\frac{1}{2n}\right)2n\sigma^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2n}$
> 
> $Var(\bar{X_{2}})=Var\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}Var(X_{i})$ assuming samples are independent
> $\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sigma^{2}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)n\sigma^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}$
> 
> I would choose $\bar{X_{1}}$ as both means are the same and unbiased, and $\bar{X_{1}}$ has lower variance.

## 3

Let $X$ and $Y$ be bivariate normal random variables with:

$$
\begin{align}
\mu_{X}&=2 \\
\mu_{Y}&=3 \\
\sigma^{2}_{X}&=1.44 \\
\sigma^{2}_{Y}&=4.84 \\
\rho&=0.6 \\
W&=0.5X+2Y
\end{align}
$$

Find:

### a

The distribution of $W$

> [!answer]
> Since a linear combination of normally distributed random variables is also normally distributed, $W$ is normally distributed.
> 
> $E(W)=E(0.5X+2Y)=0.5E(X)+2E(Y)$
> $=0.5\mu_{X}+2\mu_{Y}=0.5(2)+2(3)=1+6=7$
> 
> $Cov(X,Y)=\rho\sigma_{X}\sigma_{Y}$
> $Var(W)=Var(0.5X+2Y)=0.5^{2}Var(X)+2^{2}Var(Y)+2(0.5)(2)Cov(X,Y)$
> $=0.5^{2}(1.44)+2^{2}(4.84)+2(0.5)(2)\rho\sigma_{X}\sigma_{Y}$
> $=0.5^{2}(1.44)+2^{2}(4.84)+2(0.6)\sqrt{(1.44)(4.84)}$
> $=22.888$
> 
> $\therefore W\sim N(7, 22.888)$

### b

$P (W > 7)$

> [!answer]
> $P(W>7)=P(Z>\frac{7-7}{\sqrt{22.888}})=P(Z>0)$ by $Z$ transform
> $=P(Z<0)$ by $Z$ symmetry
> $=0.5$ by $Z$ table

## 4

Let $X_{1}$, $X_{2}$, $X_{3}$ be independent random variables such that:

$$
\begin{align}
E(X_{1})&=1 \\
E(X_{2})&=-3 \\
E(X_{3})&=5 \\
Var(X_{1})&=4 \\
Var(X_{2})&=1 \\
Var(X_{3})&=6.25
\end{align}
$$

Find:

### a

$E(3X_{1} + 2X_{2} - 0.5X_{3})$

> [!answer]
> $=3E(X_{1})+2E(X_{2})-0.5E(X_{3})$
> $=3(1)+2(-3)-0.5(5)$
> $=-5.5$

### b

$Var(3X_{1} + 2X_{2} - 0.5X_{3})$

> [!answer]
> $=3^{2}Var(X_{1})+2^{2}Var(X_{2})+(-0.5)^{2}Var(X_{3})$
> $=3^{2}(4)+2^{2}(1)+(-0.5)^{2}(6.25)$
> $=41.5625$

## 5

Suppose $X_{1}$ and $X_{2}$ are independent with the same but unknown mean $\mu$ and variances, with point estimators:

$$
\begin{align}
Var(X_{1})&=15 \\
Var(X_{2})&=4 \\
\hat{\mu_{1}}&=\frac{X_{1}}{2}+\frac{X_{2}}{2} \\
\hat{\mu_{2}}&=\frac{X_{1}}{4}+\frac{3X_{2}}{4} \\
\hat{\mu_{3}}&=\frac{X_{1}}{6}+\frac{X_{2}}{3}+9
\end{align}
$$

### a

Are any of the estimators unbiased?

> [!answer]
> $E(\hat{\mu_{1}})=E\left(\frac{X_{1}}{2}+\frac{X_{2}}{2}\right)=0.5E(X_{1})+0.5E(X_{2})=0.5(\mu)+0.5(\mu)=\mu$
> $E(\hat{\mu_{2}})=E\left(\frac{X_{1}}{4}+\frac{3X_{2}}{2}\right)=0.25E(X_{1})+0.75E(X_{2})=0.25\mu+0.75\mu=\mu$
> $E(\hat{\mu_{3}})=E\left(\frac{X_{1}}{6}+\frac{X_{2}}{3}+9\right)=\frac{E(X_{1})}{6}+\frac{E(X_{2})}{3}+9=\frac{\mu}{6}+\frac{\mu}{3}+9=\frac{\mu}{2}+9$
> 
> Yes, $\hat{\mu_{3}}$ is biased as its expectation is not equal to $\mu$.

### b

Which one has the smallest variance?

> [!answer]
> $Var(\hat{\mu_{1}})=Var\left(\frac{X_{1}}{2}+\frac{X_{2}}{2}\right)=0.5^{2}E(X_{1})+0.5^{2}E(X_{2})=0.5^{2}(15)+0.5^{2}(4)=\frac{19}{4}$
> $Var(\hat{\mu_{2}})=Var\left(\frac{X_{1}}{4}+\frac{3X_{2}}{2}\right)=0.25^{2}E(X_{1})+0.75^{2}E(X_{2})=0.25^{2}(15)+0.75^{2}(4)=3.1875$
> $Var(\hat{\mu_{3}})=Var\left(\frac{X_{1}}{6}+\frac{X_{2}}{3}+9\right)=\frac{E(X_{1})}{6^{2}}+\frac{E(X_{2})}{3^{2}}=\frac{15}{6^{2}}+\frac{4}{3^{2}}=0.8611111111111112$
> 
> $\hat{\mu_{3}}$ has the smallest variance, but it is biased, $\hat{\mu_{2}}$ has the smallest unbiased variance.

### c

Calculate the mean square error (MSE) of the estimators.

> [!answer]
> $MSE=Var+Bias^{2}$
> $MSE(\hat{\mu_{1}})=\frac{19}{4}+0^{2}=\frac{19}{4}$
> $MSE(\hat{\mu_{2}})=3.1875+0^{2}=3.1875$
> $MSE(\hat{\mu_{3}})=0.8611111111111112+\left( 9- \frac{\mu}{2} \right)^{2}$
> 
> Depending on $\mu$, $\hat{\mu_{2}}$ or $\hat{\mu_{3}}$ could be lower (better).

## 6

Let $X_{1}$, $X_{2}$, ..., $X_{7}$ denote a random sample from a population having mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^{2}$.

Consider the following estimators of $\mu$:

$$
\begin{align}
\hat{\Theta_{1}}&=\frac{X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{7}}{7} \\
\hat{\Theta_{2}}&=\frac{2X_{1}-X_{6}+X_{4}}{2}
\end{align}
$$
### a

Is either estimator unbiased? Justify your answer.

> [!answer]
> $E(\hat{\Theta_{1}})=E\left(\frac{X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{7}}{7}\right)=\frac{1}{7}\left(E(X_{1})+E(X_{2})+\cdots+E(X_{7})\right)$
> $=\frac{1}{7}7\mu=\mu$
> 
> $E(\hat{\Theta_{1}})=E\left(\frac{2X_{1}-X_{6}+X_{4}}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(2E(X_{1})-E(X_{6})+E(X_{4})\right)$
> $=\frac{1}{2}(2\mu-\mu+\mu)=\mu$
> 
> Both estimators are unbiased.

### b

Which estimator is better, and in what sense is it better?
Hint: Calculate the MSE for each estimator.

> [!answer]
> Since both estimators are unbiased, $MSE=Var$
> 
> $Var(\hat{\Theta_{1}})=Var\left(\frac{X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{7}}{7}\right)=\frac{1}{7^{2}}\left(Var(X_{1})+Var(X_{2})+\cdots+Var(X_{7})\right)$
> $=\frac{1}{7^{2}}7\sigma^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{7}$
> 
> $Var(\hat{\Theta_{1}})=Var\left(\frac{2X_{1}-X_{6}+X_{4}}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2^{2}}\left(2^{2}Var(X_{1})+Var(X_{6})+Var(X_{4})\right)$
> $=\frac{1}{2^{2}}(2^{2}\sigma^{2}+\sigma^{2}+\sigma^{2})=\frac{3}{2}\sigma^{2}$
> 
> Since $\frac{\sigma^{2}}{7}< \frac{3}{2}\sigma^{2}$, meaning its $MSE$ is lower, $\hat{\Theta_{1}}$ is a better estimator.
