## 1

No one likes a cold seat! The smart toilet’s engineers designed a resistive heater embedded in the seat, which converts current into heat using Joule heating (like a toaster). However, the inductance of this structure is wreaking havoc on the power supply, making it so users must choose between using heat or the odor extraction fan.

### a

The magnetic field of the given structure is predicted by:

$$
\vec{\mathbf{B}}=\hat z\frac{2\mu_{0}}{\pi D}\left( \ln \left( \frac{8D}{d} \right)-2 \right)I
$$

This model assumes $\mathbf{B}$ is constant over the whole region (it’s not) and that there is no magnetic field in the resistive wires (not true), but it’s good enough for toilet problems.

For $N = 2$ turns, $D = 30$ cm, $d = 3$ mm, and $I = 10 \text{A}$, predict the heater coil inductance.

> [!answer]
> $L=\frac{N(\vec B\cdot A)}{I}$
> $B\cdot A=\frac{8\pi\cdot 10^{-7}}{\pi 0.3}\left( \ln \left( \frac{240}{0.3} \right)-2 \right)10(\pi 0.15^{2})$
> $=\frac{8\cdot 10^{-7}}{0.3}\left( \ln \left(800\right)-2 \right)10(\pi 0.15^{2})$
> $L=1.766057014627458~\text{mH}$

## 3

A circular-loop TV antenna with $0.02~\text{m}^{2}$ area is in the presence of a uniform-amplitude $300~\text{Mhz}$ signal. When oriented for maximum response, the loop develops an emf with a peak value of $30$ (mV). What is the peak magnitude of $B$ of the incident wave?

> [!answer]
> $\text{emf}(t)=-B_{0}A\omega\cos(\omega t)$
> $B_{0}=\frac{30~\text{mV}}{A\omega}=795.7747154594768~\text{pT}$

## 4

The electromagnetic generator shown in Fig. 6-12 is connected to an electric bulb with a resistance of $150~\ohm$. If the loop area is $0.1~\text{m}^{2}$ and it rotates at $3600$ revolutions per minute in a uniform magnetic flux density $B_{0} = 0.4~\text{T}$, determine the amplitude of the current generated in the light bulb.

> [!answer]
> $3600~\text{rpm}=60~\text{Hz}$
> $s(t)=B_{0}A\sin(\omega t)$
> $\text{emf}(t)=-B_{0}A\omega\cos(\omega t)$
> $V=0.4(0.1)(2\pi(60))=15.079644737231009~\text{V}$
> $I=\frac{V}{R}=0.10053096491487339~\text{A}$
