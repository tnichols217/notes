# Lab 2 Report

## Introduction

The goal of this laboratory exercise was to design, model, and construct an inductive wireless power transfer system capable of lighting a micro-LED receiver at a distance of at least 50 mm. The system operates at a fixed frequency of $220~\text{kHz}$, necessitating a resonant $LC$ circuit to maximize current throughput. The primary challenge was the extremely small size of the receiver coil ($2.9~\text{mm}$ diameter), which requires a high magnetic flux density to induce the $1.2\text{V}-1.8\text{V}$ required to illuminate the LED.

## Experimental Process

The design followed an iterative optimization process:

1. **Hand Calculation:** We utilized the standard solenoid inductance formula to predict inductance of our coils before we build anything, and we later refine our calculations with the complete Biot-Savart Law adaptation for coils with the solutions of elliptic curve approximations. Our calculations are shown later in the data section.
2. **Computational Modeling:** A SciPy-based Python model was used to simulate the magnetic field strength ($B$) at various distances ($z$). We accounted for the Skin Effect, which increases the effective AC resistance of the $0.7~\text{mm}$ wire at high frequencies. We used this in conjunction with the standard capacitance values in order to decide on multiple prototype candidates.
3. **Prototype:** We built the theoretical prototypes and their associated components.
4. **Testing:** We then tested additional ideas that were unmodelled but may account for an improve performance based on observations.
5. **Data Collection:** After comparing and finalizing the prototypes, we chose a 10x 6 turn inductor with a radius of 4.8cm and a 100nF capacitor. This yielded our furthest recorded distance of 98mm from the center of the coil. We at this point also compare our calculated values with real world effects. We additionally used another coil to measure voltage induced at a distance with an oscilloscope.

## Theory

Biot-Savart Law (on-axis):
$$
B(z)=\frac{\mu_{0}NIR^{2}}{2(R^{2}+z^{2})^{3/2}}
$$

Biot-Savart for off axis coil (known solution):
Where $K$ and $E$ are the complete elliptic curves of the first and second kind

$$
\begin{align}
m&=1-\frac{4R\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{(R+\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}})^{2}+z^{2}}\\
B&=\frac{NI}{2\pi\sqrt{(R+\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}})^{2}+z^{2}}}\left(K\left(m\right)+\frac{R^{2}-x^{2}+y^{2}-z^{2}}{(R-\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}})^{2}+z^{2}}E\left(m\right)\right)
\end{align}
$$

Faraday's Law:
$$
V_{ind}=2\pi fN_{rec}A_{rec}B(z)
$$

Resonance Condition:
$$
L=\frac{1}{C(2\pi f)^{2}}
$$

Solenoid Inductance:
$$
L=\frac{\mu_{0}N^{2}A}{d}
$$

## Graphs and Data

For our initial guess of 7 turns and 45mm radius, our estimated inductance according to the solenoid equation would be $78.45~\micro H$.

$L=\frac{\mu_{0}(7)^{2}(0.045^{2}\pi)}{0.005}\approx 78.45~\micro H$

We additionally tested a 30 turn inductor with the same other parameters, which has an expected inductance of $1~mH$, but found that it has not much of an effect on the maximum distance of the LED.

Lastly we tested a 10x 6 turns inductor in an effort to keep inductance low while increasing our magnetic field output. This would have an expected inductance per coil of $57~\micro H$ and a total combined inductance of $5.7~\micro H$. In practice, we found this to roughly be $5.2~\micro H$, which lines up with our optimizer later for a capacitor of $100~nF$.

Our optimization script identified that the number of turns ($N$) and wire resistance ($R_{wire}$) largely cancel each other out at resonance. Therefore, the Coil Radius became the most critical geometric variable, however the wire resistance does play a role in how much current we can draw.

## Experimental Results

Our final design had a maximum distance above our coil of 176 mm, a maximum distance at 90 degrees to be nearly above the coil at a height of 133 mm, and a maximal horizontal distance from the center of the coil to be 168 mm, which lines up with our expectations.

## Conclusion

The project successfully demonstrated that distance is maximized when the transmitter radius is optimized for the target distance $R\approx \frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}$. For a micro-receiver of 2.9 mm, field "concentration" is more vital than raw power. By using the 10nF capacitor, we achieved a resonant state that allows the system to reach the 50 mm goal. Axial alignment remains the most critical user variable, as the tiny receiver area captures very little flux if moved off-center. We optimized this by utilizing multiple coils in parallel to increase our B field while keeping inductance low.

## Code Used

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import minimize_scalar, fsolve

# Physical Constants
mu0 = 4 * np.pi * 1e-7
rho_copper = 1.68e-8          # Ohm m
v_supply   = 5.0              # V
freq       = 220000           # Hz
omega      = 2 * np.pi * freq # rad/s

# Wire Specs
d_wire    = 0.7 * 1e-3                    # m
r_w       = d_wire / 2
# res_per_m = rho_copper / (np.pi * r_w**2) # Ohm/m
skin_depth = np.sqrt(rho_copper / (np.pi * freq * mu0))
res_per_m = rho_copper / (2 * np.pi * r_w * skin_depth)

# Receiver Specs, on the given LED. Guesswork
led_coil_diameter = 2.9 * 1e-3            # m
r_rec           = led_coil_diameter / 2
n_rec           = 150
a_rec           = np.pi * (r_rec**2)
v_led_threshold = 1.2                      # V

capacitors = {
    "100nF": 100e-9,
    "47nF": 47e-9,
    "10nF": 10e-9,
    "3.3nF": 3.3e-9,
}


def get_n_turns(l_target, r):
    # Solenoid inductance: L = (mu0 * n^2 * pi * r) / 2  =>  n = sqrt(2L / (mu0 * pi * r))
    return np.sqrt((2 * l_target) / (mu0 * np.pi * r))

def calculate_z_numerical(n, r):
    # Transmitter coil resistance
    wire_len = n * 2 * np.pi * r         # m
    r_total  = res_per_m * wire_len      # Ohm
    current  = v_supply / r_total        # A

    def find_root(z_val):
        B     = (mu0 * n * current * r**2) / (2 * (r**2 + z_val**2)**1.5)  # Biot-Savart for magnetic field strength
        v_ind = omega * n_rec * a_rec * B                                  # Faraday's Law for induced voltage
        return v_ind - v_led_threshold

    return fsolve(find_root, x0=0.01)[0]  # m

print(f"Wire Resistance (AC): {res_per_m:.4f} Ohm/m")
print(f"Receiver Diameter: {r_rec*2000:.1f} mm")
print("-" * 75)
print(f"{'Capacitor':<10} | {'Target L (uH)':<15} | {'Opt R (mm)':<12} | {'Opt N':<8} | {'Max Z (mm)':<10}")
print("-" * 75)

for name, c_val in capacitors.items():
    # Resonant inductance for this capacitor at operating frequency
    l_target = 1 / (c_val * omega**2)    # H

    def objective(r_val):
        return -calculate_z_numerical(get_n_turns(l_target, r_val), r_val)

    # Search transmitter radius: 10 mm to 60 mm
    res = minimize_scalar(objective, bounds=(0.01, 0.06), method='bounded')
    opt_r, max_z = res.x, -res.fun
    opt_n = get_n_turns(l_target, opt_r)

    print(f"{name:<10} | {l_target*1e6:>15.2f} | {opt_r*1000:>12.1f} | {int(opt_n):>8} | {max_z*1000:>10.1f}")


c_val    = 100e-9                              # 100nF capacitor
l_target = 1 / (c_val * omega**2)              # H
opt_r    = 0.0447                              # m, from optimization
opt_n    = get_n_turns(l_target, opt_r)

wire_length      = opt_n * 2 * np.pi * opt_r  # m
total_resistance = wire_length * res_per_m     # Ohm
peak_current     = v_supply / total_resistance # A

print("\nBuild:")
print(f"Number of Turns (N): {int(opt_n)}")
print(f"Wire Length: {wire_length:.3f} meters")
print(f"Total Resistance: {total_resistance:.3f} Ohms")
print(f"Peak Current: {peak_current:.2f} Amps")
```
