### 4.2.1 Synthesis of Periodic Signals

Each signal given below represents one period of a periodic signal with period $T_{0}$.

For each of these two signals, do the following:

- Sketch the signal on the interval $[0,T_{0}]$.
- Compute the Fourier series expansion in the form
$$s(t)=a_{0}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}\sin(2\pi kf_{0}t+\theta_{k})$$

#### 1

Period $T_{0}=2$, For $t\in[0,2]:s(t)=\text{rect}\left( t- \frac{1}{2} \right)$

> [!answer]
> ![[3/tables#^1a]]
> 
> $f_{0}=\frac{1}{T_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}$
> 
> Since this function is odd, all terms will be sine terms.
> 
> $a_{0}=\frac{1}{2}$
> $a_{k}=0$
> $b_{k}=2f_{0}\int\limits_{0}^{2}s(t)\sin(2\pi kt)~dt=2f_{0}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\sin(2\pi kt)~dt=\frac{1}{\pi k}(1-(-1)^{k})$
> $s(t)=\frac{1}{2}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi k}(1-(-1)^{k})\sin(\pi kt)$
> 
> ![[3/tables#^1b]]

#### 2

Period $T_{0}=1$, For $t\in\left[  -\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right]:s(t)=\text{rect}(2t)- \frac{1}{2}$

> [!answer]
> ![[3/tables#^2a]]
> 
> $f_{0}=\frac{1}{T_{0}}=1$
> 
> Since this function is even, all the terms will be cosine terms.
> 
> $a_{0}=0$
> $b_{k}=0$
> $a_{k}=2f_{0}\int\limits_{-1/2}^{1/2}s(t)\cos(2\pi kt)~dt=2f_{0}\int\limits_{-1/4}^{1/4}s(t)\cos(2\pi kt)~dt=\frac{2}{\pi k}\sin(\frac{\pi k}{2})$
> $s(t)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi k}\sin(\frac{\pi k}{2})\cos(2\pi kt)$
> $=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi k}\sin(\frac{\pi k}{2})\sin(2\pi kt+\frac{\pi}{2})$
> 
> ![[3/tables#^2b]]

### 4.4.2 Modulation Property

Hand in plots of the output of the Spectrum Analyzer for each signal.

What effect does changing the modulating frequency have on the spectral density?

Why does the spectrum have a comb structure and what is the spectral distance between impulses? Why?

What would happen to the spectral density if the period of the triangle pulse were to increase toward infinity? (in the limit)

> [!answer]
> ![[3/3_q442.svg#inv_L]]
> 
> Changing the modulating frequency shifts the larger peak over to that of the modulating frequency. For each of the 10Hz modulations, the peak of the comb is at 10Hz, and similarly for that of the 15Hz modulation.
> 
> The comb structure corresponds to the transmission of the modulated frequency, with each smaller peak being spread out by the frequency of the modulated signal itself. This is due to the fact that multiplied signals have a convolution of their frequencies, leading to the combed shape.
> 
> The density of the spectral density would increase as when the period of the pulse wave increases, its frequency decreases, making the comb structure tighter. As the period approaches infinity, the comb would be infinitely dense. The increase in density of the spectral density would in turn reduce the maximum amplitude of spectral density.

`plotNet.m`
```matlab
% Plot Network Analysis given axes, time, input samples, output samples, and number of samples to plot
function plotNet(pl, t, ins, outs, N)
    % Find total samples and Ts
    Nn = numel(ins);
    Ts = (t(Nn) - t(1))/Nn;

    % Calculate FFTs of inputs and outputs
    outsf = fft(outs);
    insf = fft(ins);

    % Calculate our transfer function
    transfer = outsf./insf;
    Fs = 1/Ts;
    % Resample our x-axis for frequency
    f = (0:N-1) * (Fs/Nn);

    % Plot Magnitude and Phase of the response
    pls = nexttile(pl);
    plot(pls, f(1:(N/2)), abs( transfer(1:(N/2))) );
    xlabel(pls, 'Frequency in Hertz');
    title(pls, 'Magnitude of Frequency Response');

    pls = nexttile(pl);
    plot(pls, f(1:(N/2)), angle( transfer(1:(N/2))) );
    xlabel(pls, 'Frequency in Hertz');
    title(pls, 'Phase of Frequency Response');

    % Plot our Impulse response using ifft
    pls = nexttile(pl);
    h = ifft(transfer);
    h=h/Ts;
    plot(pls, t(1:(round(Nn/2))), real(h(1:(round(Nn/2)))) );
    xlabel(pls, 'Time in Seconds');
    title(pls, 'Impulse Response');
end
```

`plotSpec.m`
```matlab
% Plot Spectran Analysis given axes, time, signal, number of items to plot, and title
function plotSpec(pl, t, signal, N, ti)
    % Find total samples and Ts
    Nn = numel(signal);
    Ts = (t(Nn) - t(1))/Nn;

    % Plot Signal itself
    pls = nexttile(pl);
    plot(pls, t(1:2*N), signal(1:2*N));
    xlabel(pls, 'Time in seconds');
    ylabel(pls, 'Amplitude');
    title(pls, ti);

    % Calculate the amplitude of the FFT
    X = abs(fft( hamming(Nn).*signal ));
    f = (1/Ts)*((1:Nn)-1)./Nn;

    % Plot FFT analysis
    pls = nexttile(pl);
    plot(pls, f(1:N),X(1:N));
    xlabel(pls, 'Frequency in Hertz');
    ylabel(pls, 'Spectral Density');
    title(pls, 'Spectral Density Plot');
end
```
