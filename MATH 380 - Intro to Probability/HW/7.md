## 4.6

A pollster would like to estimate the fraction $p$ of people in a population who intend to vote for a particular candidate. How large must a random sample be in order to be at least $95\%$ certain that the fraction $\hat p$ of positive answers in the sample is within $0.02$ of the true $\hat p$?

> [!answer]
> Assuming the distribution is roughly normal,
> 
> $ME=z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}}$
> $n=\left(\frac{z_{\alpha/2}}{ME}\right)^{2}p(1-p)$
> 
> We can maximise $p$ by setting it to $0.5$ to provide the most conservative estimate for $n$.
> 
> By rounding up, this provides us with a conservative estimate of $2401$.

## 4.10

A hockey player scores at least one goal in roughly half of his games. How would you estimate the percentage of games where he scores a hat-trick (three goals)?

> [!answer]
> If this player scores at least one goal for half of his games, then approximating with Poisson, our $\lambda$ should be $\ln 2$.
> 
> This means that the 3 or more goals would be represented by $P(\text{Poisson}(\ln 2)\ge 3)$
> 
> Which is approximately equal to $3.331315624047693\%$ of games.

## 4.24

We roll a fair die repeatedly and keep track of the observed frequencies of the outcomes $1,2,\dots,6$.

### a

Show that the probability of seeing at least $17\%$ fours converges to zero as the number of rolls tends to infinity.

> [!answer]
> $p=\frac{1}{6}$
> $\hat p_{n}=\frac{\text{Binomial}(p, n)}{n}$
> $P_{n}=P(\hat p_{n}\ge0.17)$
> 
> We can approximate $p_{n}$ with the normal distribution
> 
> $p_{n}'\sim \frac{N(np, \sqrt{np(1-p)})}{n}$
> $=N\left( p, \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}} \right)$
> 
> As $n\to\infty$ we can see that $p_{n}'\to N(p, 0)$
> Therefore $P(p_{n}'>0.17)\to0$ as well

### b

Let $A_{n}$ be the event that after $n$ rolls, the frequencies of all six outcomes are between $16\%$ and $17\%$. Show that for a large enough number of rolls, the probability of $A_{n}$ is at least $0.999$.

> [!answer]
> $A_{n}=P(0.16<p_{n}'<0.17)$
> 
> Since as $n\to\infty$, $p_{n}'\to N(p,0)$
> 
> $P(0.16<p_{n\to\infty}'<0.17)\to 1$
> 
> For $n=10^{500}$
> $P(0.16<p_{n}<0.17)\approx 1$
> 
> On a 64-bit floating point computer, our STD is $0$ and the computer incorrectly states that our probability _is_ $1$.
> 
> `STD: 0.0000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000037267799624996494`
> `P(0.16 < pn < 0.17): 1`

## 4.26

$100$ randomly chosen individuals were interviewed to estimate the unknown fraction $p\in(0,1)$ of the population that prefers whole milk to skim milk. The resulting estimate is $\hat p$. With what level of confidence can we state that the true $p$ lies in the interval $(\hat p-0.1,\hat p+0.1)$?

> [!answer]
> Sampling from a population with $p$ who prefer whole milk to skim milk with a size of $100$ gives us the following distribution:
> 
> $S\sim\text{Binomial}(p, 100)$
> $\approx N\left( p, \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{100}} \right)$
> 
> The most conservative estimate for our STD. would be at $p=0.5$:
> 
> $\sigma=\frac{1}{20}$
> 
> $2P\left( Z< \frac{20}{10} \right)\approx95.4\%$
> 
> So we are $95\%$ confident.
