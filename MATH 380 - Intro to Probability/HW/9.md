## 6.6

Suppose that $X$, $Y$ are jointly continuous with joint probability density function

$f(x,y)=\begin{cases}xe^{-x(1+y)}&x>0,y>0\\0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$

### a

Find the marginal density functions of $X$ and $Y$.

> [!answer]
> $f_{X}(x)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}xe^{-x(1+y)}~dy=xe^{-x}x^{-1}=e^{-x}$
> $f_{Y}(y)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}xe^{-x(1+y)}~dx=(y+1)^{-2}$

### b

Calculate the expectation $E[XY]$

> [!answer]
> $E[XY]=E[X~E[Y|X]]=E\left[ \frac{X}{X} \right]=1$

### c

Calculate the expectation $E\left[ \frac{X}{1+Y} \right]$

> [!answer]
> $\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+y}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}e^{-x(1+y)}~dx~dy=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}2(1+y)^{-4}~dy=\frac{2}{3}$

Calculate the expectations $E[X]$ and $E[Y]$

> $E[Y]=\frac{1}{3}$

Calculate the expectation $E[XY]$

## 6.11

Let $X$ have density function

$f_{X}(x)=\begin{cases}2x&0<x<1\\0&\text{else}\end{cases}$

and let $Y$ be uniformly distributed on the interval $(1,2)$. Assume $X$ and $Y$ are independent. Give the joint density function of $(X,Y)$. Calculate the probability $P\left( Y-X\ge \frac{3}{2} \right)$.

> [!answer]
> $f_{X,Y}(x, y)=\begin{cases}2x&0<x<1,1\le y\le 2\\0&\text{else}\end{cases}$
> 
> $\int\limits_{0}^{1/2}\int\limits_{x+ \frac{3}{2}}^{2}2x~dy~dx=\int\limits_{0}^{1/2}2x\left( \frac{1}{2}-x \right)~dx=\frac{1}{8}-\frac{2}{24}=\frac{1}{24}$

## 6.18

Suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are integer valued random variables with joint probability mass function given by

$p_{X,Y}(a,b)=\begin{cases} \frac{1}{4a}&1\le b\le a\le 4\\0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$

### a

Show that this is indeed a joint probability mass function

> [!answer]
> $\sum\limits_{a=1}^{4}\sum\limits_{b=1}^{a} \frac{1}{4a}=\sum\limits_{a=1}^{4}\frac{1}{4}=1$

### b

Find the marginal probability mass functions of $X$ and $Y$

> [!answer]
> $p_{X}(x)=\frac{1}{4}$
> $p_{Y}(y)=\left[ \frac{25}{28}, \frac{13}{48}, \frac{7}{48}, \frac{3}{48} \right]$

### c

Find $P(X=Y+1)$

> [!answer]
> $\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{16}=\frac{13}{48}$

## 7.4

Suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are independent exponential random variables with parameters $\lambda\ne\mu$. Find the density function of $X+Y$

> [!answer]
> $\int\limits_{0}^{s}\lambda e^{-\lambda x}\mu e^{-\mu(s-x)}~dx=\lambda\mu e^{-\mu s}\frac{1-e^{(\mu - \lambda)s}}{\lambda-\mu}=\frac{\lambda\mu}{\lambda-\mu}(e^{-\mu s}-e^{-\lambda s})$

## 7.6
