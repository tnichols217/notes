## 9.7

A car insurance company has $2500$ policy holders. The expected claim paid to a policy holder during a year is $\$1000$ with a standard deviation of $\$900$. What premium should the company charge each policy holder to assure that with probability $0.999$, the premium income will cover the cost of the claims? Compute the answer both with Chebyshev's inequality and with the CLT.

> [!answer]
> $P\sim N(1000, 900)$ by CLT.
> $P(S_{Pn}>\text{price})=0.999\implies x=3.09$
> $S_{Pn}\sim N(2500*1000,900*\sqrt{2500})$
> $2500*1000+3.09*900*\sqrt{2500}=2639050$
> $\implies \text{price}>1055.62$
> 
> By Chebyshev's,
> $0.001=\frac{900*\sqrt{2500}}{\text{price}^{2}}$
> $\text{extra price}>1423024.9470757707$
> $\text{price}>1569.2099788303083$

## 9.12

Let $X$ be a $\text{Poisson}(2)$ random variable, and $Y$ an independent $\text{Unif}(0,1)$ random variable.

### a

Use Markov's inequality to bound $P(X+Y>10)$.

> [!answer]
> $X$ and $Y$ are independent, $E[X+Y]=2+0.5=2.5$
> $\text{Var}(X+Y)=2+ \frac{1}{12}=\frac{25}{12}$
> 
> Using Markov,
> $P(X+Y>10)=\frac{2.5}{10}=0.25$

### b

Use Chebychev's inequality to bound $P(X+Y>10)$.

> [!answer]
> $\frac{\frac{25}{12}}{7.5^{2}}=0.037$

## 10.4

Let $N$ have a $\text{Bin}\left( 100, \frac{1}{4} \right)$ distribution. Given that $N=n$, flip a fair coin $n$ times, and let $X$ be the number of heads observed.

### a

Find the conditional distribution of $X$ given that $N=n$. Be precise about the possible values and a proper condition probability mass function.

> [!answer]
> $X\sim\text{Binom}(n, 0.5)$
> $P(X=x|N=n)=\begin{pmatrix}n\\x\end{pmatrix}\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{n}$
> 
> The support is $[0,n]\in\mathbb Z$

### b

Find $E[X|N=n]$ for all the values of $n$ that it is defined.

> [!answer]
> $E[\text{Binom}(n, 0.5)]=0.5n,n\in[0,100]\in\mathbb Z$

### c

Use part b to compute $E[X]$

> [!answer]
> $E[X]=E\left[ \frac{N}{2} \right]=\frac{1}{2}E[N]=\frac{25}{2}=12.5$

## 10.6

Let the joint density function of $(X,Y)$ be

$f(x,y)= \frac{x+y}{4},0<x<y<2$.

### a

Find $f_{X|Y}(x|y)$

> [!answer]
> $f_{Y}(y)=\int\limits_{0}^{y}\frac{x+y}{4}~dx=\frac{3y^{2}}{8}$
> $f_{X|Y}(x|y)=\frac{\frac{x+y}{4}}{\frac{3y^{2}}{8}}=\frac{2(x+y)}{3y^{2}},0<x<y<2$

### b

Compute the conditional probabilities $P\left( X< \frac{1}{2}|Y=1 \right)$ and $P\left( X< \frac{3}{2}|Y=1 \right)$.

> [!answer]
> $P\left( X< \frac{1}{2}|Y=1 \right)=\int\limits_{0}^{1/2} \frac{2}{3}(x+1)=\frac{2}{3} \frac{5}{8}=\frac{5}{12}$
> 
> $P\left( X< \frac{3}{2}|Y=1 \right)=1$ because $X<Y$

### c

Compute the conditional expectation $E[X^{2}|Y=y]$. Check the averaging identity

$\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}E[X^{2}|Y=y]f_{Y}(t)~dy=E[X^{2}]$

> [!answer]
> $=\int\limits_{0}^{y} \frac{2}{3}x^{2} \frac{x+y}{y^{2}}~dx=\frac{2}{3}y^{2} \left( \frac{y^{4}}{4}+\frac{y^{4}}{3} \right)=\frac{7y^{2}}{18}$
> 
> $\int\limits_{0}^{2} \frac{7y^{4}}{48}~dy=\frac{14}{15}$
> $\int\limits_{0}^{2}\int\limits_{0}^{y}x^{2} \frac{x+y}{4}~dx~dy=\int\limits_{0}^{2} \frac{7y^{4}}{48}~dy=\frac{14}{15}$

## 10.15

Consider a large insurance company with two types of policies: policy A and policy B. Suppose that the number of claims the company sees in a given day has a Poisson distribution with a parameter of $12$. Suppose further that a randomly selected claim is from a type A policy with probability $\frac{1}{4}$.

### a

Find the probability that the company will receive at least $5$ claims from A policies tomorrow.

> [!answer]
> $A\sim\text{Poisson}\left( \frac{12}{4}=3 \right)$
> $B\sim\text{Poisson}\left( \frac{12(3)}{4}=9 \right)$
> $P(A\ge 5)=0.18474$

### b

Find the probability that the company will receive at least $5$ claims from B policies tomorrow.

> [!answer]
> $P(B\ge 9)=0.54434$

### c

Find the probability that the company will receive at least $10$ total claims tomorrow.

> [!answer]
> $T\sim\text{Poisson}(12)$
> $P(T\ge 10)=0.75761$

## 10.38

We generate a random variable $Y\sim\text{Exp}(1)$. Then we generate another exponential random variable $X$ whose parameter is the value $Y$ just observed.

### a

Find the joint density function of $(X,Y)$.

> [!answer]
> $Y\sim\text{Exp}(1)$
> $X\sim\text{Exp}(Y)$
> 
> $f_{X,Y}(x,y)=f_{X|Y}(x|y)f_{Y}(y)=e^{-y}(ye^{-yx})=ye^{-(x+1)y}, x>0,y>0$

### b

Suppose we observe $X=x$, How is $Y$ now distributed? Identify the distribution by name.

> [!answer]
> 
> $f_{Y|X}(y|x)=\frac{f_{X,Y}(x,y)}{f_{X}(x)}\propto f_{X,Y}(x,y)$
> $\sim\text{Gamma}(2, x+1)$
