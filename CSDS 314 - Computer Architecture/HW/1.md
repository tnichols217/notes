## 3

Two processors that implement the same ISA must have identical performance for the same program

> [!answer]
> False, different clock speeds, caching topology, or physical size of node can all influence performance when running on a physical processor.

## 4

In a system optimized for high throughput, the elapsed time of an individual program may increase even though overall system performance improves.

> [!answer]
> True, high throughout optimization often means processing multiple things at once with a scheduler, which means that sometimes single threaded or single process tasks can take longer due to constant context switching.

## 5

A system with a lower average CPI is guaranteed to have better performance than a system with a higher CPI

> [!answer]
> Just because your CPI is low doesn't necessarily mean that its clock speed is comparable too. If a higher CPI system has a significantly higher clock speed, then it may still be faster than the low CPI system.

## 6

Consider two different implementations of the same instruction set architecture. The instructions can be divided into four classes according to their CPI (class A, B, C, and D). P1 with a clock rate of 2.5 GHz and CPIs of 1, 2, 3, and 3, and P2 with a clock rate of 3 GHz and CPIs of 2, 2, 2, and 2. Given a program with a dynamic instruction count of one million instructions divided into classes as follows: 10% class A, 20% class B, 50% class C, and 20% class D, which is faster: P1 or P2?

### a

What is the global CPI for each implementation?

> [!answer]
> $G_{CPIP1}=1(0.1)+2(0.2)+3(0.5)+3(0.2)=2.6$
> $G_{CPIP2}=2(0.1)+2(0.2)+2(0.5)+2(0.2)=2.0$

### b

Find the clock cycles required in both cases.

> [!answer]
> $1000000(2.6)=2600000$
> $1000000(2.0)=2000000$
> 
> $P_{2}$ is faster.
